# Design decisions

Start with the audience's desired result and the real thing being offered. Choose a visual object or example that expresses it. A download should feel useful and tangible without inventing extra assets or results.

Use a clear hierarchy: headline, main visual, necessary labels, CTA. Let the visual carry the idea. Avoid turning the full post into small text on a poster. At phone size, the offer and familiar tools should register first.

Typography is a design choice for this piece. Consider geometric sans, condensed display, editorial serif, rounded sans or another suitable family. Change family, weight, case and spacing deliberately. A brand's type system takes priority when the brief is on-brand. Ensure selected fonts actually load in the export.

Choose a palette from the message, brand and references. The options are broader than warm paper/green or black/lime. Use colour to separate meaningful elements, with enough contrast for readable labels. When comparing palette/type options on an accepted layout, keep composition, copy and animation consistent so the difference is easy to judge.

Use original SVG or CSS shapes for code-native objects. Use the environment's image-generation tool for raster artwork when needed; do not claim a stock or AI-created image is an actual product screenshot. Never draw fake familiar logos from memory. Respect trademark and asset licensing conditions.

Keep all relevant tool marks visible. Make space for moving objects instead of fading the logos or covering them. Keep motion calm and purposeful. A book with turning pages implies a book or guide; use an install/activation treatment for a plugin.

Show the resource's true contents. Keep the full operational method in the resource when the goal is an opt-in. Use genuine examples and supported compatibility claims, not invented prices, client results, deadline pressure or guarantees.
